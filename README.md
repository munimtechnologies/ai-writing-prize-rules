# ai-writing-prize-rules

Which writing prizes accept work made with generative AI, and exactly what each one asks you to disclose. One row per prize in [`prizes.csv`](prizes.csv), with the rule quoted word for word in its original language, a link to the page it came from and the date it was checked.

Made by MV2 of Munim, Inc., with AI agents working in his name. Data and text: CC BY 4.0. Code (`check_prizes.py`, `verify_quotes.py` and their tests): Apache-2.0.

**Not legal advice.** Rules change between editions. Read the organiser's own page before you enter.

## What the rules show

- **Disclosure goes in six different places**: an entry form (Prix Pascal, Hoshi, plus Sogen's checkbox), the end of the synopsis (Sogen, Hayakawa), the author's biography (Bungei), a tag on the work (both Kakuyomu divisions), a separate statement or report sent with the work (AI Philosophy Competition, Prompty Awards, both FC2 prizes, and the Korea AI Creative Literature Award's entry email) and the manuscript's cover page (Matsumoto Seicho, item 10 of the cover sheet: whether AI was used and how). A writer who reuses one disclosure for every prize will put it in the wrong place for most of them.
- **The same kind of statement can be hidden from the judges, read by them, or scored.** The AI Philosophy Competition's methodology report is kept from the judges and published afterwards. Prix Pascal says its AI details inform the jury and are "pas un critère d'élimination". FC2 puts its AI question in the optional note that goes to the screeners and the judge, and asks entrants to "explain how the use of AI supports your artistic project": there the disclosure is part of the artistic case. The Korea AI Creative Literature Award goes furthest: a prompt note and an account of the creative process are part of every entry, and the originality of AI use and the collaboration process count for 30% of the judging.
- **"Accepts AI" covers very different rules.** The AI Philosophy Competition and the Prompty Awards require the work to be mostly AI-generated (Prompty sets the bar at 50% of the final prose). The Korea AI Creative Literature Award requires work made with AI tools or in collaboration with AI, but sets no share. Hoshi accepts non-human entrants if a contactable guardian or agent is named, yet says AI-generated text must not be used as is and must be revised by a person. Sogen accepts mostly-AI stories but only one per entrant. Matsumoto Seicho's examples of AI use are help with wording, proofreading and translation, but the rule sets no limit. FC2's rule begins "if any part of the manuscript ... was generated by AI" and sets no limit either.
- **Five of the thirteen ask for your records or can ask for them** (Sogen, Hayakawa, Hoshi and Prompty may request prompts, outputs or other documentation; the Korea AI Creative Literature Award wants a prompt note with every entry). Keep them from the first draft.
- **The biggest prizes are not AI contests.** The largest sums here, Matsumoto Seicho's ¥5,000,000, FC2 Doctorow's US$15,000 and Hayakawa's ¥1,000,000, come from established literary prizes that ask entrants to say how they used AI. None of these rules, as quoted, sets a limit on how much of the text AI may write. The contests built around AI (AI Philosophy Competition, Prix Pascal, Prompty Awards, Korea AI Creative Literature Award) offer smaller or unstated sums, and two of them charge entry fees (Prompty US$25 to US$40, the Korea award ₩20,000 per entry).
- **Most open doors are in Japan, but not all.** Seven of the thirteen prizes here that accept AI-made work take entries in Japanese. In English, FC2's two innovative-fiction prizes (an imprint of the University of Alabama Press) accept disclosed AI-generated text, while Writers of the Future and the Nebula Awards ban it. All three English-language fiction contests here that accept it charge an entry fee (FC2 US$25, with a fee-waived form; Prompty US$25 early, US$40 final), and FC2 Doctorow is only for writers with at least three published books.
- **In Korean, one publisher's page shows two wordings of a ban.** The one Korean contest here that accepts AI requires it. The publisher Changbi lists six contests on one rules page. Three of them, its new-writer prize, its young-adult fiction prize (₩20,000,000 advance) and its children's new-writer prize, carry the same sentence: "AI 제작 작품은 응모할 수 없습니다." ("AI-made works may not be entered"). Its children's-book manuscript contest (₩20,000,000 advance per category) words it differently: entries found to be written with plagiarism or generative AI "심사 대상에서 제외되거나 당선이 취소될 수 있습니다" (may be excluded from judging or have the award cancelled). That is a discretionary rule, not an automatic one, and it is listed here as `banned` because it allows no AI-written manuscripts. The page's text gives no entry rules, and so no AI rule, for Changbi's story contest or its picture-book prize, and neither is listed here.
- This is a small, hand-picked list, not a survey, so it shows where to look rather than how common each policy is.

## How it was checked

On 2026-11-01 at 06:13 UTC, `verify_quotes.py` fetched every source page and looked for every sentence of every quote in the page's visible text. All 16 rows of v0.4.0 were found exactly as written (`exact`). The run also saved each page's visible text (`--save-text`). The three Changbi rows added in v0.5.0 come from the same saved text of Changbi's rules page, fetched in that run: their sentences were found there character for character, the ban sentence three times (once in each of the three contests' notes) and the children's-book sentence once.

- Seventeen rows cite the organiser's own page; the two FC2 rows cite the organiser's own entry forms on Submittable. Two rows cite contest listings. Sogen's comes from kobonabi.com, which reprints the rules, because the publisher's site (tsogen.co.jp) refused automated requests; its official page is https://www.tsogen.co.jp/award/sfss/18th/. The Korea AI Creative Literature Award's comes from linkareer.com, a Korean contest listing that marks the notice as verified by the organiser's contact.
- Changbi's page could only be read without a verified TLS certificate (`--insecure-retry`), so the report marks its check `unverified`. The text matched, but whoever re-checks should know the connection was not authenticated. This applies to all four Changbi rows.
- Changbi's young-adult prize lists its dates with the labels swapped: "마감" (deadline) next to 2027-01-04 midnight and "공모 오픈" (opening) next to 2027-03-31 17:59:59. Its entry section says the entry site opens on 2027-01-04, so the row reads the window as 2027-01-04 to 2027-03-31 and says so in `deadline_note`.
- The Prompty Awards' fees and prizes are from the contest's home page (https://promptyawards.com/); its rules page gives none.
- Prizes checked and **left out** because their quotes could not be confirmed: the CBC Short Story Prize 2027 (its rules page timed out), Naver Series' 2026 web-novel contest (its notice page is built by JavaScript, so the rule text is not in the page that was fetched) and the 2026 Unslop AI-written fiction prize from Hyperstition AI (US$10,000; its page is built by JavaScript, and the rule text found so far comes only from third-party quotes). They will be added only once a fetch finds the exact text.

## Re-checking

```
python check_prizes.py                    # header, required fields, allowed values, dates, https sources, unique ids, consistent banned rows
python verify_quotes.py --out-dir out     # fetch each source once and look for each quote; writes out/VERIFY.md and out/verify.json
python -m unittest                        # offline tests (no network)
```

`verify_quotes.py` reports each sentence at one of four levels: `exact` (as written, after HTML entities and whitespace are normalised), `in_source` (only in scripts or embedded data, not in the visible text), `folded` (only after Unicode NFKC, case and quote-mark folding and removing whitespace, for example a line break inside a Japanese sentence) or `missing`. A row is `exact` only if every sentence is. It sends an honest User-Agent naming this repository, fetches each URL once with a one-second pause between pages, and exits 1 if anything is not exact (use `--report-only` to always exit 0). `--insecure-retry` reads a page whose certificate cannot be verified and marks it `unverified`; `--save-text` keeps each page's visible text in `out/pages/`.
